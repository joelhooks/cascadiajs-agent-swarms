# Humans in the Swarm

Source: Joel voice note, 2026-05-01.

## Raw thought

The talk is about building agentic structures: building and harnessing the power of multiple agents that can coordinate and communicate together.

ATProto enters as the communication/identity substrate:

- how do agents communicate?
- what does communication even mean for agents?
- how do you know if thousands of agents are working on similar problems?
- how do agents coordinate around a mission?

In the Dungeons & Dragons cartridge, the problem is the quest. Agents coordinate with a leader and different party roles to complete the mission.

The mission can be fictitious, but the structure generalizes:

- different agents
- different prompts
- different roles
- different communication patterns
- different work policies
- shared goal accomplishment

LLM agents are only one aspect. Human actors are also in the mix.

## Key question

Where do humans come into this, and how do they participate in the overall play, work, or goal accomplishment?

## Emerging answer

Humans are not outside the swarm. Humans are high-context participants in the swarm fabric.

They contribute:

- mission definition
- taste and judgment
- approval for risky/irreversible actions
- ambiguity resolution
- social trust and accountability
- final acceptance of work
- occasional direct execution

## Game mapping

In D&D terms:

- human as dungeon master: defines world, rules, constraints, consequences
- human as party leader: sets mission and coordinates roles
- human as player character: participates in the mission directly
- human as referee/reviewer: accepts or rejects outcomes
- human as patron/client: commissions work and judges value

## Software-work mapping

In OpenClaw-style work:

- human sets the mission
- swarm decomposes into units of work
- agents claim/attempt work
- artifacts are produced in isolated workspaces
- review loops inspect and merge/reject
- humans intervene at decision boundaries

## Talk implication

Do not frame swarms as “AI replacing humans.” Wrong fight, boring fight.

Frame them as multiplayer systems where humans and artificial agents coordinate through explicit protocols and artifacts.

Potential line:

> The question is not whether humans are in the loop. The question is what role humans play in the party.
