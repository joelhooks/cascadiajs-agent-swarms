# Play as Method

Source: Joel voice note, 2026-05-01.

## Core point

The talk is ultimately trying to encourage and demonstrate **play to do something useful**.

The technical implementation may be useful and interesting, but the deeper message is that playing with tools is a legitimate way to explore the frontier of modern agentic coding.

## Thesis seed

Play is not the opposite of useful work. Play is how you discover the rules of a new medium before the professional practices have hardened.

## What Joel wants to show

- a concrete example of how he explored agent swarms
- a demonstration that is fun, weird, and technically real
- a path from playful fiction/game mechanics to useful software-work structures
- permission for developers to mess around with the tools instead of waiting for best practices
- a way to build bespoke systems that fit a situation instead of copying generic chatbot workflows

## Why games work

Games make system structure visible:

- roles
- rules
- state
- resources
- goals
- consequences
- communication
- turn-taking
- collaboration
- failure
- replay

These are also the structures agentic coding systems need.

## Possible line

> Play is how you learn the affordances of a new medium before anyone has named the patterns.

## Talk implication

Do not over-index on proving the architecture is “serious.” The playfulness is the point. Agents of Catan and D&D cartridges are not gimmicks if they reveal real structures for coordinating agentic work.

The audience should leave thinking:

> I can use play to understand these tools, build strange prototypes, and discover useful systems I would not have designed from a blank PRD.
